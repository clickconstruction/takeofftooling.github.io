Takeoff Tooling · alpha

Test script for the pre-alpha build

Every route below changed in the pre-alpha review (Sept 7–14). Work through it in order — the bid you build in Round 1 is the one Rounds 2 and 3 use. Budget about forty minutes for Rounds 1–2, plus ten on a real phone. Where a figure is printed, compare it to the screen: this app's failure mode is a wrong dollar amount that looks right.

App: takeofftooling.com Build main @ 93e914b Sept 14, 2026

Set up in thirty seconds

Open the app in a private / incognito window on a desktop browser. The app saves into that browser, so a private window guarantees a clean first-run and throws the test bid away when you close it. Don't sign in yet — Round 5 covers that. Keep a note open: for anything that looks wrong, write down which screen, what you did, what you expected, what you saw, plus the numbers and your browser width.

R1 Ten minutes that have to pass

If anything here fails, stop and report it — the rest of the script is built on this bid.

  1. Open the app cold

    Load the URL in the private window.

    What right looks likeA line reading Start here. above one empty row. No totals panel yet — it appears once a row has a description. No error banner, nothing flashing.

  2. Build one priced line

    Description 2x4 LED Troffer. Click the type… chip and pick Lighting (or press L). Quantity 24, Labor 0.75, Price 89.50. Then set the Labor rate to 92.

    What right looks like — check all five Lighting materials $2,148.00 · Sales tax (%) field pre-filled 8.25 giving $177.21 · Labor 18.00 hrs · Labor total $1,656.00 · Grand Total $3,981.21.

  3. Type a number, then immediately click something elsewas broken

    Click into the Quantity cell, type 25, and without pressing Tab click straight into the Labor rate box.

    What right looks likeThe click lands — the cursor is in the Labor rate box — and the quantity keeps 25. Until this week the click was swallowed and the typed number was lost. Try it a few times, and on the Price and Labor cells too.

  4. Correct the bid with the purchase list openwas broken

    Set the quantity back to 24. Press Generate purchase list (PO), then — with the list on screen — change the quantity to 30.

    What right looks likeThe list updates as you type: 30 and $2,685.00. The list total and the summary never disagree. The list used to freeze the moment you started correcting.

  5. Undo right after typingwas broken

    Type a different quantity, then press Undo in the header (or ⌘Z / Ctrl-Z with the cursor outside a field).

    What right looks likeUndo is lit and works on the first press. It used to stay greyed out until something else redrew the page. Set the quantity back to 24 before moving on.

  6. Print for review

    Print Options → Print for review. Open the PDF.

    What right looks likePage 1 is the summary you just read on screen, with dollars and cents. Page 2 is the line items — every description sits inside its own column, nothing overlaps the Quantity or Price columns, hours show two decimals, and the file is named after the job and today's date.

R2 The routes that changed

Keep the same bid. Each block is independent — if one fails, note it and carry on to the next.

Counts arriving from Count Tooling

  1. A re-count must not duplicate the fixturewas broken

    Copy these two lines, then press Paste from CountTooling.com:

    Clipboard2x4 LED Troffer ⇥ 30 ⇥ E2.1
    LED Wall Pack - WP1 ⇥ 7 ⇥ E1.0
    (tab-separated — type it into a text file if pasting is awkward)

    What right looks likeOne primary button reading Update 1 count · add 1 fixture. Press it: the troffer row becomes 30 and keeps its price and hours — there is no second troffer row. The wall pack arrives typed as Lighting. The old "Add All" turned 24 + 30 into 54.

  2. A count that went down

    Paste again with the troffer at 18.

    What right looks likeThe preview shows × 18 · was 30, −12 and the button takes it. A lower count is a real correction, not something to ignore. Put it back to 24 afterwards.

A device run

  1. Quantities are totals for the whole line

    Add a row: Duplex receptacle 20A, quantity 20, type Devices — the editor opens by itself.

    What right looks likeEight chips ("+ Box", "+ Cover"…), not eight empty tables. Click + Box: the new row is seeded at 20, not 1, and the column header reads Quantity (all 20 runs). The panel prints the extended price and the per-run price.

  2. Fill a row from the book

    On that box row press Book, search 1900 box, and add 4" Square Box, 1-1/2" deep.

    What right looks likeThe description is exactly the part name — no section name pasted onto the end. Hours 0.25 and price 3.90 come with it. Then Save parts to the bid: Devices materials $78.00, Devices labor 5.00 hrs.

A conduit run

  1. Nothing reaches the bid until you press Savewas broken

    Add 3/4" EMT Homerun, quantity 150, price 1.10, type Conduit. In the wizard: quick-add Dirt to 24in, add + BACKHOE, Next, pick a 90° Elbow preset and set quantity 4 / labor 0.12 / price 2.40, then press Back, then Cancel.

    What right looks likeCancel asks first — "Discard unsaved changes in this editor?". Say no, go forward again, set overage to 10%, and Save parts to the bid. The old wizard wrote to the bid at every step, and Back-then-Cancel threw work away silently.

  2. Trenching and rentals are not stock

    Read the totals panel, then the purchase list.

    What right looks likeConduit materials $191.10. The trench ($2,250.00) and the backhoe sit under Other charges → Site work & rentals, are not taxed, and are not on the purchase list — nobody orders a backhoe from the supply house. The conduit line on the list reads 165 · 3/4" EMT Homerun (incl. 10% overage), one line, not two.

  3. Change the run afterwards

    On the manifest, change the homerun's quantity from 150 to 200.

    What right looks likeThe overage child follows on its own — 20 ft, not the stale 15. It used to keep the old number until you re-opened and re-saved the wizard.

Finding a part

  1. Four searches that used to return nothingwas broken

    Open Labor & Price Book and search, one at a time: 2p 20a breaker · exit sign · mac adapter · single pole switch.

    What right looks likeAll four return hits, sorted with the closest match first, under three honest headings: Your book (hours), MC assemblies (hours + material), Supply house · Elliot (prices). Try the words you'd actually say — anything that returns nothing is worth reporting.

  2. The assemblies side

    Switch to Assemblies and use the filter box; open Abbreviation Key.

    What right looks likeThe filter narrows the tree (it did not exist before). The key decodes the codes you can actually see on screen — nf, n3r, gd and the rest.

Proving the number

  1. A row spun down to zerochanged on purpose

    Spin the troffer's quantity down to 0 and read the totals and the purchase list. Then put it back to 24.

    What right looks likeAt zero the row contributes nothing — no material, no hours, no purchase line — and its quantity cell greys out. It used to bill one unit, and the purchase list dropped it, so the two reports disagreed. This changes the total on any old bid that used a zero row: say so if that surprises you.

  2. One material bought at two prices

    Add a second row with the same description as an existing one, but a different price, then regenerate the list.

    What right looks likeOne line, with the unit column showing a range (e.g. $89.50–$92.00) and the extended column showing the exact money. Quantity × unit no longer has to multiply out, and the list total still matches the summary to the cent.

Delivering it

  1. The share link carries the ratewas broken

    Print Options → Copy share link. Open it in a different browser (or a second private window).

    What right looks likeThe copy opens with a banner saying it's a copy, and the same Grand Total as the sender. The rate didn't travel, so one bid showed three different totals depending on who opened it. Open the same link twice: you get your copy back, not a second identical bid.

  2. The other two prints

    Under Print Options, fill in Job details (client, address, permit). Then Print purchase list (PO) and Print with form.

    What right looks likeThe PO PDF is the purchase list you see on screen — quantities, unit prices, extended costs and a materials total. The form prints your job details on page 1. The details stay with this bid: switch bids and they don't follow you.

Several bids at once

  1. Name, rename, archive

    Use the project switcher: name this bid, make a second one, rename one from the switcher, then open Manage projects and archive it.

    What right looks likeNaming a job closes the dialog (it used to sit there and mint duplicates on a second Enter). The header shows Saved just now. Lines counts components, not just top rows. An archived bid leaves the switcher, which then reads 1 archived.

  2. Switch bids from inside an editorwas broken

    Open a conduit or device editor, type something, and — without saving — switch to the other bid from the header.

    What right looks likeIt asks about the unsaved edit, and whichever you choose you land on a working manifest. This used to leave a blank page, and on a duplicated bid it could save your work into the wrong job.

R3 On a real phone or tablet

Ten minutes on actual hardware — a desktop window shrunk down is not the same test. Same URL.

  1. The bid on a phone

    Open the bid, type a five-digit quantity like 12000, tap the +/− spinners, and scroll the whole page.

    What right looks likeRows are stacked cards. The quantity is fully readable. Nothing scrolls sideways. Tapping a field does not zoom the page. The trash icon is on every row without hunting for it.

  2. The book and the editors on a phonewas broken

    Open the Labor & Price Book and search; then open a device run.

    What right looks likeYou can read every part's name — search results and supply-house rows alike. At phone width the name was clipped to about three characters with no way to pan. In the editor, Save parts to the bid is pinned at the bottom of the screen instead of a full page-length away.

R4 Offline, and installed

  1. Reload with no signalnew

    With the app open, turn on airplane mode (or switch off Wi-Fi) and reload the page.

    What right looks likeThe app comes back with your bid, instead of the browser's "no internet" page. Typing, totals and the Parts side of the book all keep working. The Assemblies side needs one online visit first, and says so plainly.

  2. Add to home screen

    On the phone: Share → Add to Home Screen. On desktop Chrome: the install icon in the address bar.

    What right looks likeIt opens in its own window with an icon, no browser address bar.

R5 Signed in

The account side is fully set up

Everything this round depends on is live as of Sept 14 — sync, the conflict-safe project write, and the password-reset address. If "Forgot your password?" does nothing, that is now a real bug worth writing up.

  1. Sign in and sync

    Sign in with the emailed six-digit code. Then open the app on a second device and sign in there.

    What right looks likeYour bids arrive on the second device rather than an empty table. The header says when it last saved. Edit the same bid on both machines and neither copy vanishes — if they collide you get a second bid named (conflict — time) rather than losing one.

  2. Set a password

    While signed in, open the Cloud dialog and use Set a password. Sign out and back in with it.

    What right looks likeAn account created by emailed code can give itself a password without an admin handing one over.

  3. Forgot your password?new

    Sign out. On the sign-in form, type your email and press Forgot your password?. Open the email.

    What right looks likeThe email carries a link (not a code). It opens the app itself — takeofftooling.com, not some other page — showing a "choose a new password" form. Set one, and sign in with it.

R6 Changed on purpose — please don't file these

All of these are deliberate. If any of them looks wrong as a decision, that's worth saying — but they aren't defects.

  • A row at quantity 0 bills nothingIt used to count as one unit. This changes the total of any old bid that parked a row at zero.
  • Sales tax starts at 8.25%It's now a field on each bid rather than a fixed 8.5%, and it travels with a share link.
  • Trenching and rentals are Other chargesUntaxed, and off the purchase list. Sand, patch and pole bases are still stock.
  • "Book", not "PB"Same button, plain name.
  • The trash is on every rowThe "Remove items" mode in the ☰ menu is gone.
  • The chip's text opens the type pickerThe pencil still opens the run's editor in one click; the old × is gone (pick "No type" instead).
  • Print with form has no pop-upJob details live under Print Options and belong to the bid.
  • Hours print to two decimals0.75 hrs no longer rounds to 0.8 beside a price that used the exact figure.
  • Permit hours countLabor typed on a Permits / Power co. / Temporary power row now counts in the labor total, on screen and in the PDF.
  • One row per part in "Your book"Where two curated rows named the same part, the priced, assembly-hours row stayed — a 20A single-pole switch reads 0.45 h / $12.50, not 0.20 h. Searching "1900 box" still finds the 4" square box.

R7 Already known

  • Two "occupancy sensor" rows"Your book" has an Occupancy sensor, wall switch under Occupancy Sensors and an Occupancy Sensor Switch under Switches. Deliberately left for now; not a bug.

Anything that produces a wrong number — a total, an hour count, a purchase line — is the highest-value thing you can find, even if you can't reproduce it. Note the figures you saw and roughly what you'd done; the exact steps can be worked out afterwards. Ticks on this page are saved in your own browser only.
