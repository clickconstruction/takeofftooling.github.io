# Your first bid in five minutes

One fixture, typed in, given a type, priced, and totalled. Nothing to install,
nothing to set up, no Save button to hunt for. If you have counts waiting in
Count Tooling, start with [From count to bid](from-count-to-bid.md) instead —
this article assumes you are typing the first line yourself.

## Put one fixture on the bid

1. **Open the app.** You land on a bid called *Untitled project* with one blank
   row already on it and a dashed line above the table reading *Start here.* The
   totals panel stays out of the way until there is something to total.

2. **Name the job.** Click the bid's name in the header. The menu offers
   **Rename**, **New project** and **Manage projects…**. Rename changes the name
   of the bid you are looking at — that is what you want on the first one. Type
   the job name and press Enter.

3. **Type the fixture.** Click the description box on the blank row and type what
   it is — `2x4 LED Troffer - A1`. The row is already at quantity 1, so it is
   worth something the moment it has a name; the *Start here* line goes away and
   the totals panel opens underneath.

4. **Give it a type.** Click **type…** in the Type column and pick **Lighting**,
   or just press `L`. The six letters are `L` lighting, `G` gear, `D` devices,
   `C` conduit, `W` wire, `S` special systems; **Other ▾** at the bottom holds
   permits, power-company charges and temporary power. Picking a plain type
   drops the cursor straight into that row's Labor box.

   Lighting, Gear, Special Systems and the three Other types stay on the table.
   **Devices, Conduit and Wire open their own editor** the moment you pick them —
   that is where the boxes, covers, trenching and waste live. See
   [Parts under a device run](parts-under-a-device-run.md) and
   [Conduit and wire runs](conduit-runs.md).

5. **Type the hours and the price.** **Labor is hours per unit** — the hours for
   *one* of them, not for the line. A troffer you hang in 45 minutes is `0.75`.
   Price is the price of one, before tax. Plan Page is free text — `E2.1`, or
   *Break room*, whatever gets you back to the sheet.

   > **What you'll see:** with quantity 1 at 0.75 hrs and $89.50, the totals read
   > Lighting **$89.50**, Sales tax (8.5%) **$7.61**, Materials TOTAL
   > **$97.11**, and Lighting hours **0.75**. About eight seconds from an empty
   > app, on a phone as well as a desktop.

6. **Set the labor rate.** The rate lives in the totals panel, in the LABOR
   column, under *Labor TOTAL (hrs)*. Type your loaded shop rate — the dollars
   per hour you bill, not what you pay. It is saved with the bid.

   > **What you'll see:** at `85`, Labor Total $ becomes **$63.75** (0.75 × 85)
   > and the Grand Total at the bottom reads **$160.86**.

7. **Add the next line.** **Add Row** at the bottom of the table adds a row and
   puts the cursor in its description; so does pressing Enter in the last box of
   a row. Type `200A Panel - MDP`, press `G` for Gear, click **+** twice to get
   to 3, and fill in 4 hours and $1,250.

   > **What you'll see:** Gear **$3,750.00**, Sub Total **$3,839.50**, sales tax
   > **$326.36**, Materials TOTAL **$4,165.86**, labour 12.75 hrs = **$1,083.75**,
   > Grand Total **$5,249.61**. Every figure adds up to the cent — check one with
   > a calculator once and you will not need to again.

That is a bid. Read it over in [Check the bid before it goes
out](checking-the-bid.md), then print or send it: **Print Options** under the
totals holds *Print for review*, *Print purchase list (PO)*, *Print with form*
and **Copy share link**.

## Changing a type

The chip in the Type column is two controls. The **pencil** on a Devices,
Conduit or Wire chip is the one-click door back into that row's editor. The
chip's **text** re-opens the type picker, with **No type** at the bottom if you
want the row to carry no type at all.

A row with no type still counts — its money lands on a **Misc.** line in the
totals, so nothing goes missing while you decide.

## Good to know

- **There is no Save button, and you do not need one.** Every keystroke on the
  table is written to this browser about half a second after you stop typing,
  and again when you close the tab. Under the bid's name in the header is the
  time of the last save — *Saved just now*, *2 min ago*.
- **A project is a bid.** One job, one project. The header name is the open one;
  the menu switches between them, and **Manage projects…** renames, duplicates
  and deletes. You cannot delete the bid you have open — open another first.
- **Labor is hours per unit.** Quantity 24 at 0.75 hrs is 18 hours, and the
  totals panel shows the 18.
- **Quantity 0 is none of it.** No material, no hours, no purchase line. Spinning
  a row down to 0 is a clean what-if; it is not how you delete a row. See
  [Shaping the bid](shaping-the-bid.md).
- **Sales tax is the job's own rate**, typed beside the labor rate. A new bid
  starts at 8.5%. It travels with a share link, so the person who opens the link
  reads the same grand total you do.
- **Undo and Redo cover the bid table only** — not the price book, not saved
  assemblies, not the labor rate. Ctrl/Cmd-Z outside a box does the same thing as
  the header button; inside a box, your browser's own undo is left alone.
- **Hours print to two places and money carries thousands separators**, so the
  hours and the dollars beside them always agree.
- Everything is stored in this browser. Signing in is optional and is for
  getting the same bid onto a second computer — see
  [Saving, signing in, and two computers](saving-and-signing-in.md).
