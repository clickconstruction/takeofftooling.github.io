# On a tablet or a phone

Same site, same URL, same bid. On an iPad at the counter or a phone on the walk
back to the truck, the app rearranges itself rather than shrinking.

There is one number that decides everything: **900 pixels wide**.

## Turn the iPad sideways to get the office table

- **Portrait** (a 9th-gen iPad is 768 px) — the bid is a stack of cards, one per
  line. Every field is labelled: **Qty**, **Labor**, **Price**, **Plan page**.
  Nothing is cut off and nothing scrolls sideways.
- **Landscape** (1024 px) — the desktop table comes back, all eight columns,
  exactly as it looks on the office monitor.

Rotating is the switch. There is no setting.

> **What you'll see:** a wire run of 12,000 ft reads **12000** in the quantity box
> on a phone — the whole number, not the first three digits — and Labor, Price and
> Plan page each sit on their own line instead of running into each other.

## Working with a finger

- The **−** and **+** on a quantity are 44 by 44 points on a touch screen — a
  thumb's worth, not a mouse pointer's. The book icon, the remove icon and the
  fitting buttons get the same treatment.
- Every input is at least 16 pt on a touch device, which is the size iPhone Safari
  needs to stop zooming the page when you tap into a field. Tap a quantity, type,
  tap the next one: no pinching.
- Number fields bring up the number pad, not the full keyboard.

## In a run editor

Open Devices, Conduit or Wire on a phone and the parts tables are wider than the
screen — deliberately. Each section is its own **sideways-scrolling strip**: swipe
the row left to reach Price and the trash can, and the page itself never moves.

**Save is always on screen.** Below 900 px, the Save/Cancel bar is pinned to the
bottom of the window, so **Save parts to the bid** (or **Save and Back to
Manifest** in the conduit and wire flows) is one tap away no matter how far down
the parts you have scrolled. On an iPad in landscape the bar is not pinned — it
sits at the foot of the page, like the desktop.

**Load into Ledger** — the button that fills a run from a saved assembly — is on
screen and tappable at phone width. It used to lay itself out past the edge.

## The book on a small screen

Open the Labor & Price Book and the part rows are **cards**, not a seven-column
table crushed into 375 px. The part's name gets a full line of its own; the part
number, the price, the **In use** pill and the **Add** button follow underneath.

Both halves work at phone width — your own sections and the supply-house
sections, and the search box at the top that looks through everything at once.

> **What you'll see:** search *3/4 EMT coupling* on a phone and every result reads
> as a name you can act on, with the section it came from and its numbers on the
> line below.

Tap a row's **In use** pill and the part card opens over the book — the whole
card, no scrolling needed — so you can record what the counter just quoted you
while you are standing at the counter. See
[Recording a quote](recording-a-quote.md).

## Counts arriving from Count Tooling

Open a Count Tooling `#import=` link on the phone and the preview fits: one
column instead of two, and the primary button — **Add 2 fixtures**, or **Update 1
count · add 1 fixture** on a re-count — entirely on screen where you can press it.
This is the most likely reason a phone ever opens this app, and it is the screen
that used to be half off the edge.

## Away from signal

The whole loop works offline: open the app, edit, price from your own book, read
the total, print.

- **Reload it with no bars and it still comes up.**
- **Add it to your home screen** from your browser's share menu and it opens as
  its own app, without the address bar.
- The **Assemblies** side of the book is a large download and needs one
  connection before it is available offline. Until then it says so.

## Good to know

- The switcher menu, **Manage projects** and every modal stay inside the screen at
  375 px — the Manage projects rows stack into cards, and nothing hides behind a
  sideways scroll you have to discover.
- The layout is decided by width, not by which way you are holding the device. An
  iPad Pro 12.9" in portrait is wide enough for the table; a smaller iPad is not.
- Split View on an iPad follows the same rule: a half-width window gets the phone
  layout, two-thirds gets the cards.
- The `G L D C W S` letters on the book's tabs are keyboard shortcuts. They do
  nothing on a touch screen — tap the tab.
- A supply-house section shows its first 100 parts with **Show all N parts**
  underneath. On a tablet that is tens of thousands of rows and it will crawl:
  use the filter box instead.
- The type picker, the part card and the print options all fit a phone; a bid
  that arrives by share link opens on a phone the same as anywhere else.
